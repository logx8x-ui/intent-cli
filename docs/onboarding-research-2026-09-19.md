# Intent onboarding: rationale, copy and first-user test

Research checked 19 September 2026. This records the research rationale and source-aligned flow, not a claim that a novice usability test has passed. The final source review below is read-only; build and live results belong in the implementation verification report. The current brief supersedes earlier AI-assisted onboarding: one personally chosen purpose, exactly three skills, no AI interpretation or new analytics service.

## Decisions and evidence

| Decision | What the source supports | Limit / application to Intent |
|---|---|---|
| Let people perform useful actions; keep help beside the target; offer skip and replay | Apple's [Onboarding HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/onboarding?changes=_7) recommends interactive, brief, optional onboarding, contextual tips and postponing nonessential setup. | Platform guidance, not an Intent conversion experiment. Use the actual picker, marking and graph actions; avoid a second tutorial-only implementation. |
| Teach one action at a time and observe the result | Apple's [Teaching Through Tutorials](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/onboarding-for-games/) recommends introducing basic elements progressively and allowing people to demonstrate them before later objectives. | Game guidance transferred as a design hypothesis to a Mac utility. Success must follow real product events, not animations, timers or clicking “Next.” |
| Use concrete, conversational instructions and action-labelled buttons | Apple's [Writing for interfaces](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2022/10037/) advocates clear, helpful language considered as part of interface design. | It does not establish a best-performing sentence. Keep the brief's first question; test comprehension with actual newcomers. |
| Explain permissions when the benefit is visible | Apple's [Privacy HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/privacy?changes=_10_7) recommends requests when the feature needs them, with a specific explanation of use. | Purpose entry needs neither a profile nor capture permission. Before real previews/marking, explain Screen Recording and Accessibility separately. A settings window opening is not a successful grant. |
| Finish with a recognisable cue and a simple action | [Gollwitzer & Sheeran (2006)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0065260106380021), *Implementation Intentions and Goal Achievement*, synthesised 94 independent tests and found a positive effect on goal attainment from specifying when/where/how to act. | Research supports cue–action planning in the studied settings, not this app's retention, a three-minute promise, or a fixed effect on installs. Our proposed cue is starting a task on the Mac; the action is the actual Intent shortcut. |
| Reinforce repeat use without claiming a habit exists | [Lally et al. (2010)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ejsp.674), *How are habits formed*, followed self-chosen eating, drinking or activity behaviours repeated in a consistent context for 12 weeks. Automaticity increased with repetition and varied substantially between people; one missed opportunity did not materially derail the process. | Observational/self-report health behaviour evidence, not an app trial. A tutorial completion is not automaticity or task completion. Do not promise a universal number of days, add guilt or demand a streak. |
| Verify real accessibility workflows, not labels alone | Apple's [Performing accessibility testing for your app](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/accessibility/performing-accessibility-testing-for-your-app?changes=lat_3_1_4__5_3_8_5&language=objc) recommends a task/device/settings matrix and completing tasks with assistive technologies. [VoiceOver evaluation criteria](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-accessibility/voiceover-evaluation-criteria) requires usable access to common tasks. | Test purpose entry, picker selection, marking, optional save and exit with keyboard/VoiceOver; check contrast, reduced motion/transparency and focus order. A compilation or automated audit is insufficient. |

**Our hypotheses, not research findings:** approximately three minutes is a pacing budget; a small countdown may orient some people but distract or pressure others. None of the reviewed sources establishes a countdown as optimal. Preserve the requested visible countdown, make it secondary, allow unlimited continuation and test reactions. A relevant purpose may make the setup feel useful; measure that instead of asserting improved retention.

Research access: Apple guidance and publisher abstracts were read through the web tool. The Gollwitzer/Sheeran publisher page also exposed the introduction; the complete chapter and Lally's complete article were not inspected. No conversion statistics or habit duration guarantee is inferred from those abstracts. An attempted NYU publication-page fetch failed; the publisher source above supports the actual research summary.

## Verified interaction map before implementation

- `GlobalHotKeyManager` and `QuickMarkGesture`: bare **`** invokes the overview when idle; during an active run it hides/restores eligible running controls. Double **`** stages a target. Use this existing router, not an onboarding event tap. Render configured shortcut labels from their actual source; do not imply every binding is user-configurable.
- `QuickSelectionController.markForeground` / `QuickSelection.toggleNativeTabGroup`: double **`** toggles the foreground native window, or the browser's highlighted tab group (normally just the current tab). When the entire group is already marked, repeating removes it; otherwise all group members are added. **Hold ` + Tab** marks the browser window's current tabs. Browser Guard and permissions are prerequisites, not optional proof of success.
- Picker **Return** starts the selection; **/** changes Allow/Block there. Staged selection uses **hold ` + Return** to run and **hold ` + B** to change mode. App-only and browser-tab guidance must reflect the selected target.
- `IntentAppModel.endAndSaveActiveSession` uses the normal finish/save path; `savePurposeSessionCandidate` writes to the existing graph through `OnboardingSavePlan`. Its internal name does not justify exposing Purpose Mode. Saving applies the latest edited answer. Repeated saves reuse the existing identity; changing an earlier saved setup requires an explicit Update action and preserves its identity, canvas position and folder.
- `markForeground` and `runMarked` refuse active sessions. Therefore teaching the second mode after a real first run requires an explicit normal end chosen by the user; closing tutorial UI must never end that run automatically.

## One purpose, exactly three skills

The sequence below reflects the implemented user-facing copy and interaction choices after review. `{purpose}`, `{overview}`, `{finish}` and `{finishAndSave}` represent retained user text or labels from the real shortcut configuration. Keep the first question unchanged, preserve its Unicode wording, trim only surrounding whitespace and allow edits. Do not submit partially composed IME text.

**Entry, not a skill.** “Make room for what you came to do.” Secondary: “Set up one real intention, learn two ways to start, and save it if you want.” / “Around 3 minutes. Take the time you need.” Primary **Let’s begin**; secondary **Not now**. The initial button starts the countdown and opens the first question: **“What do you want to do on your Mac right now?”** One text field, with “Reply to friends, make something, watch a film… your words are enough.” Primary **Choose my setup**, disabled for empty or whitespace-only input. The answer names the draft, guides every later instruction, and is not re-entered at saving.

| Skill | Contextual copy and real action | Success evidence / next choice |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Choose your setup** | “Press {overview} once.” / “Click the apps or windows you need for ‘{purpose}’. Click a browser to choose its tabs.” **Allow selected:** “Only your selected items stay available.” **Block selected:** “Only your selected items are restricted; switch with / in the overview.” Modifications are identified along the bottom; Timer is one optional example. Return explicitly starts real restrictions. **Open the picker instead** is an accessible alternative, not evidence of shortcut use. | Observe overview opening, selected targets and the normal session-ready event. “Your intention is running.” This is a prepared environment, not completed work. Offer **End this run & try quick selection**, which explicitly uses normal finish, or **Keep working · close guide**, which leaves the intention running. Once the run has ended, **Try the quicker way** advances. |
| **2. Select where you are** | “Click a window, then double-tap `.” Explain the current tab/native selected group versus an app window, with green Allow and red Block. After a mark: “Selection detected—now hold ` and press Return.” Secondary hints identify **` + B**, **` + Tab** and **` + Esc**. Starting applies real restrictions; guide closure does not end the run. | Observe the successful mark change and real staged-selection run, plus the actual shortcut event. A key alone or failed start is not mastery. **See how to save this setup** advances while running; **Save it for next time** appears after a completed run. A completed run is recognised before checking permissions again, so recovery does not unnecessarily block saving. |
| **3. Keep a useful setup** | “Make next time one less decision.” Save uses the latest purpose. Show **Finish & save to canvas** while running or **Save to canvas** when idle; **No thanks—keep it a one-off** remains available. If a setup is already saved, show its actual name. When the current draft differs, offer **Update saved setup** (or **Finish & update saved setup**) and **Keep existing · show on canvas**. Updating while running explicitly ends that run. | Record save only after persistence succeeds. **Show it on my canvas** selects the real saved node and moves to the final cue. Updating replaces the existing stable ID only after explicit consent; replay alone never overwrites it. Record reuse only after a real saved-intention run. Skip/keep-existing do not fabricate a new save, and retries cannot create a duplicate. |

Final cue: **“When I sit down to use my Mac, I press {overview} and choose an intention.”** Saved setups offer a real **Run my saved intention** action, or current-tab reselection when required. Final button **Let me do my thing** while running, otherwise **Done for now**. Never say the user has finished their task or formed a habit. End users who stop after the first useful run can replay the remaining skills later without losing that success.

Use a small coach panel anchored clear of the real target, visible focus, one primary action and textual selected/mode state alongside green/red outlines. Explanations expand only when needed. Timer, checklist locking, advanced modes and every modification do not need demonstrations; no timer/checklist is silently attached to tutorial sessions.

## Countdown, recovery and evidence rules

- The three-minute guide clock is independent of intention limits. Show **3:00 → 0:00 → -0:01** continuously; at zero keep normal styling and optionally “Take your time.” No forced advance, alarm, false failure, or pause for permissions.
- Begin creates one attempt. Back, field editing, rerenders and permission windows retain that attempt and elapsed time. Exit retains the draft and clock; explicit Resume uses the same attempt, including the time away. Explicit Replay starts a new clock/attempt while preserving prior saved intentions. Do not auto-restart the guide on each launch. Persist elapsed/origin state and the step to return to after editing the purpose; test relaunch, sleep and civil-clock changes deliberately.
- Capture full elapsed time from Begin, including prerequisites, alongside core interaction time measured separately. Label both. Time away, skips and setup delays are not silently removed to meet the pacing budget. Explicit Browser Guard connection waits are counted as setup until a valid connection or **Continue with app windows**; normal optional disclosure browsing is not treated as required setup.
- Recovery copy: “Intent needs Screen Recording to show your windows here.” / “Intent needs Accessibility to use these shortcuts and protect your selection.” / “Browser Guard isn't connected in {browser}.” Provide the appropriate **Open Settings** or **Connect Browser Guard**, recheck actual status, and offer **Skip for now**. Mark that skill blocked/skipped, never simulated success.
- During active runs, preserve real finish/emergency controls and explain that exiting the guide leaves the intention running. Clean up only tutorial-owned, uncommitted guidance state. A saved user intention or intentionally started session is not disposable tutorial state. The selection scope retains marks that existed before the tutorial and restores them after exit when no run is active; resuming during the tutorial run cancels pending restoration until the next exit.
- Local measurement can record attempt ID, step enum, elapsed milliseconds, action source, outcome and bounded error category. Separate shortcut-recognised, target-marked, session-started, saved, reused, skipped and exited. Store no purpose text, titles, URLs or raw key stream in analytics. Existing consent/infrastructure governs return-use measurement. Event presence proves action, not understanding.

## Short uncoached first-user test

Use an isolated profile with a functioning emergency escape. Ask permission before recording; do not capture private pages. Recruit a person unfamiliar with Intent; a developer or automated walkthrough is not this test.

1. Say: “Think of something you actually want to do on your Mac. Use Intent to get ready for it. Tell me what you're thinking.” Do not name a shortcut or point at the next control.
2. Observe purpose entry, permission comprehension, first successful run and both selection approaches. If they stop or skip, record that outcome without correcting it into a success. Safety help is immediate; record it as assistance.
3. Ask: “How would you make this setup available next time?” Accept choosing not to save. Check for one saved node only if they choose saving.
4. After a short unrelated activity, ask: “You are starting another task. Show me how you would begin.” Record independent recall separately from same-session tutorial completion.
5. Ask: “What stays available in Allow? What happens in Block? What did the timer make you think would happen at zero?” Record their words and any anxiety/confusion without supplying the answer first.

Record full first-run elapsed time, core interaction time, assistance, skips, failed starts, duplicate saves and unaided repeat performance. These are initial usability observations, not statistically established retention gains. Current novice-test result: **not run**. Repeat with keyboard/VoiceOver users and varied layouts/displays; no accessibility pass is claimed by this research document.

## Source-review outcome

The follow-up read-only review confirmed corrections for edited-purpose save naming, explicit stable-ID saved-setup updates, edit-return persistence, long-purpose display limits (full text retained), completed quick-selection recovery before permission checks, explicit browser-setup measurement and scope restoration on resume. These are source findings, not physical shortcut, IME, VoiceOver, timing or novice-test passes. Canvas hit testing and final build/regression execution remain owned by the main implementation task; this document introduces no separate completion claim.
